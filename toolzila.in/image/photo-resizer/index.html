Photo and signature size for exam forms: a simple guide

Almost every online form in India asks for a passport-size photo and a scanned signature, and almost every one has its own size limit. SSC, railway (RRB), bank (IBPS, SBI), UPSC and state exam portals commonly ask for a photo of roughly 20 to 50 KB and a signature of roughly 10 to 20 KB, in JPG or JPEG format. The exact numbers change from one notification to the next, so always read the instruction box on the form you are filling and use those values.

Why is JPG the format most forms ask for?

JPG compresses photographs very well. A PNG of the same face can be five to ten times bigger, which is why a portal that wants "under 50 KB" nearly always wants JPG. JPG also opens on every phone and computer, so the exam board does not have to worry about different file types.

Why does my photo look blurry after resizing?

  • The original was small. A tiny photo stretched to a bigger size will always look soft. Start from the largest, clearest picture you have.
  • Quality was pushed too low. To fit a 20 KB limit, the file must lose detail. A bigger target such as 50 KB keeps more detail, so use the highest limit the form allows.
  • The picture was stretched. If you type both width and height, the tool fits the photo to exactly that size and does not crop. A face can look squeezed if the proportions differ from your original, so crop the photo to the right shape on your phone first.
  • Width and height were forced. If a form wants exact pixels and a very small KB size at the same time, the image has to be squeezed hard. Fix the pixels first, then see how far the size can go.
  • The photo was edited many times. Every save as JPG loses a little quality. Resize once from the original instead of resizing a resized file.

Tips for a photo that gets accepted the first time

  • Use a plain white or light background and look straight at the camera.
  • Keep the face in the centre with a little space around the head and shoulders.
  • For the signature, sign with a dark pen on plain white paper and photograph it in daylight. Crop away the empty paper.
  • Check the file size in KB after downloading, not just before. Some phones show size in MB.
  • Keep a copy of the accepted photo and signature in a folder for the next form.

Quick checklist before you upload

If you leave width and height empty, the tool may also reduce the pixel size a little to reach your KB target. If the form needs exact pixels, type them in.

  1. Read the form's rules for size in KB, pixels and format.
  2. Choose the photo or signature in this tool and set the target size.
  3. Add width and height only if the form asks for them.
  4. Download, open the file once to confirm it looks sharp, then upload.
  5. If the portal still rejects it, check the file extension is .jpg or .jpeg.
